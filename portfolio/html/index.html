Black Holes

A black hole is a region of spcetime where gravity is so intense that nothing, not even light, can escape from it. This occurs because a massive amount of matter is compressed into an extremely small volume, creating an escape velocity exeeding the speed of light.

Formation and structure

Most black holes form when massive stars exhaust their nuclear fuel and collapse under their own gravity, often following a supernova explosion. The boundary beyond which escape is impossible is called the event horizon, while the center contains a singularity--a point of infinite density where know laws of physics break down.

Types and detection

Black holes are categorized by mass: stellar-mass black holes (3-100 solar masses) result from dying stars; intermediate black holes (100-100,000 solar masses); and supermassive black holes(millioons to billions of solar masses)reside at the centers of most galaxies, including our own Sagittarius A*. Although invisible, they are detected by their gravitational effects on nearby stars, accretion disks of heated matter emitting X-rays, and ripples in spacetime known as gravitational waves.
